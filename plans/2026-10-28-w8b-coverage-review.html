W8b needs a proof revision before runtime work.

REVISE the proof S0a evidence model ready

28 October 2026 · repository gmackie/hall-monitor · program HM-16 · owning issue HM-21, In Progress · reviewer: Codex · reviewed source: 322734e56743963ffcb6a9a006d73cc8f30a3a82

The per-stream commit sequence and receiver verifier are a useful candidate architecture. The proposal does not yet establish when an occurrence bucket can be called exact, zero or final. Build isolated evidence primitives first, resolve the proof with adversarial traces, then implement the verifier before the producer.

R1 · Concrete expiry trace and acceptance tests

At bucket end B, a rollup contains E=1. Let the already-rounded rollup, coverage and gap deadlines be B+10, B+20 and B+30 time units. The proposed retention order holds. At B+11 the rollup is hidden or deleted, the span is live and there is no gap; the proposed zero predicate becomes true.

Choose a representation that persists the qualifying read floor or a bounded count-validity witness. Merely testing the current configured TTL is insufficient after long→short→long policies. Previously expired or purged data cannot be reconstructed as zero. A coverage span may have a longer storage lifetime than its permission to qualify a count.

Tests: read before alarm; actual alarm deletion; restart; nonzero and genuine-zero buckets; long→short→long and short→long; replay after deletion; legacy rows; a policy with no saved count-validity history. The last case is unavailable.

Proposal: lines 194, 414 and 516–527. Current source: apps/telemetry-worker/src/store.ts:366 excludes expired rollups before deletion; retention.ts:57 defines rollup expiry.

R2 · Replay, conflicts and bounded identity history

Checkpoint (ckSeq=5, accounted.seq=5) is created legitimately. Receiver deliveries 6–10 then advance presence to 10. Delivering or retrying checkpoint 5 now satisfies accounted.seq < verified_n without any producer rollback. A new checkpoint can also lag received deliveries when an acknowledgment was lost.

Use an explicit per-tenant/stream/epoch checkpoint identity and fingerprint. A known identical replay is idempotent; a retained identity with a different fingerprint is a conflict. Old evidence outside the retained identity window must be ignored or rejected as stale without advancing positive coverage or falsely tainting normal reordering. A newer checkpoint contradicting a previously accepted checkpoint is a separate rule from “receiver has seen more deliveries.” Specify bounded tombstones and the exact behavior after their expiry.

Tests: every permutation of two checkpoints and their deliveries; identical replay before/after newer deliveries; same checkpoint number with different bytes; an older unknown checkpoint after history expiry; a new checkpoint behind presence because of a lost ack; separate tenant, stream and epoch scopes. Never independently merge time and seq from incompatible checkpoint tuples.

Proposal: lines 248, 403–411, 609 and 615. Restore detection remains limited; normal delayed messages are not proof of a restore.

R3 and R4 · Time cuts, fences and finality

R3 trace: commit 1 at time 1 is acked; commit 2 at time 2 is pending. The written definition permits (through=3, seq=1), because all seqs ≤1 are acked and commit 1 is before time 3. It does not prove that all commits through time 3 are included. A correct frontier cannot pass an unresolved earlier commit.

R4 trace: bucket [0,1), fence F=1, accounted time 2. The proposal seals it. A request prepared at occurrence 0.5 completes at time 3 and becomes a fence gap. That disproves the proposal’s universal statement that every completion with occurrence in the bucket has commit time within the fence. The producer checks the fence for accepted deliveries; fence violations remain real completions.

S0b must choose and prove either a closure mechanism that prevents future relevant historical completions, or explicitly qualified “as of” semantics with the same sequence/time cut for counts and coverage. An evidence revision or later downgrade alone does not make an earlier claim of final exactness correct. Keep existing occurrence-based rollups and leave coverage unavailable until that choice is proven. Do not silently redefine E as only successfully delivered or fence-admitted completions.

Tests: checkpoint racing prepare/commit; pending early commit; equal logical timestamps; wall-clock rollback; allowed future skew; late fence violations with delayed checkpoints; old/new revisions; the accepted occurrence-age horizon; unresolved work at closure. Compare receiver-visible claims against a separate ground-truth commit ledger at every trace step.

Proposal: lines 201–205, 254, 478–484, 495 and 803. This review supplies counterexamples, not a replacement proof.

R5 · Keep the first slice inert

The existing parseTelemetryRetentionPolicy in packages/telemetry-contracts/src/index.ts:374 requires exactly eight keys and is used by both producer and receiver. The proposal’s S0 instruction to extend it to nine keys changes runtime admission.

S0a gets a separate experimental parseCoverageRetentionPolicy in packages/telemetry-coverage/. It may call existing pure validation helpers without altering them. A nine-key fixture must be accepted only by the experimental parser and still rejected by the existing parser. No app, sink or live contracts package may import the model.

Additional corrections and review limits
  • Intentional collection exclusions: disabled, unbound, unenrolled or unapproved telemetry currently returns no observation by design. Some paths log a coarse reason. Those are future proof boundaries, not a confirmed defect in W8a, which claims no completeness.
  • Heartbeat wording: the design document says “heartbeat and exposure inventory.” Clarify that the inventory must be authoritative for the claimed scope; the original sentence does not claim a heartbeat alone is sufficient.
  • Completion reservation: store.ts:218 does not check the affected UPDATE row count or a reservation generation. Reproduce stale/double completion before changing the hot path or claiming it is a current incident. Resolving its semantics is a prerequisite for assigning one sequence per completion in S2.
  • Precision and retention: committed_at, clock_hi and armed_last are potentially finer than 15 minutes. Enumerate their purpose, access and TTL rather than claiming all state is coarse.
  • Bounds: coalescing ranges must conservatively enlarge taint; epoch/range/span/history bounds must never discard negative evidence while leaving an exact claim. Key every identity by tenant, stream and epoch, including uniqueness constraints.
  • Unobserved config changes: observations cannot reconstruct a disabled/re-enabled interval they never saw. Define the scope and unknown state; do not bridge the interval from a later heartbeat.
  • Out of proof scope: front-door availability, student activity, product submissions, receiver storage rollback and compromised writer keys. No enrollment or dashboard claim follows from an evaluator-completion proof.

Revised implementation sequence

  1. S0a · Experimental evidence primitivesSeparate parser, checkpoint identity rules and finite evidence ledger; explicit adversarial fixtures. No final/complete/zero classifier and no runtime imports. Self-contained executable plan.
  2. S0b · Resolve and review the coverage proofCount validity, full time-prefix frontier, checkpoint conflict history, closure/as-of semantics, scope and precision/TTL contract. Produce positive and negative reachability evidence. Blocks S1.
  3. S1 · Receiver verifierStrict v3 admission, migration, presence verification, conservative bounded evidence, read-time/alarm retention and revocation. Synthetic only; existing v1/v2 unchanged.
  4. S2 · Producer evidenceFirst reproduce reservation semantics and verify platform transaction/ack guarantees. Then atomically commit one sequence/evidence item with each scoped completion. No checkpoint dispatch yet.
  5. S3–S4 · Dispatch and optional activationSeparate checkpoint/alarm delivery PR from the completion path. Optional cron only after explicit expected-stream inventory, authorization, resource bounds and cost measurement.
  6. S5 · Synthetic adversarial drillsReconcile fixed ground truth across retries, outage, reordering, restarts, policy change and rollback. Prove no transient false exactness.
  7. S6 · Approved rolloutHuman retention/privacy decisions, deployment target verification and synthetic enrollment before any real tenant. W9 controls all reader/disclosure work.

Open decisions and stop conditions

Engineering owns R1–R5, transaction semantics, durable-ack output gating, clock/fence/tick measurements, restoration limitations and storage bounds. The product/evaluation owner must confirm which four evaluation kinds and degraded completions belong in E. Authorized privacy/legal owners must approve retention values and the new evidence precision/disclosure contract; no durations are supplied by this plan.

Stop before S1 if a trace still permits false zero, false exactness or a benign replay to create a rollback taint. Stop before S2 if completion uniqueness or platform atomicity is unproven. Stop before rollout if the receiver target, retention approvals, authenticated synthetic acceptance or revocation/rollback evidence is missing.
